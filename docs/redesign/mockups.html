    Combined homepage: design notes

    The Library’s calm layout and hero, with the Field Guide’s collections as “How can I help?”, cut to five choices, and pull quotes from your posts.

    • Five needs instead of seven, each with a small icon that animates when tapped. On the real site each one is a plain link to /topics/<name>/, and the icons are inline SVG with CSS animation (under 3 KB in total, no library).
    • Every collection shows a verbatim pull quote from its first essay. Quotes live in data/collections.yaml, so you choose them.
    • The header carries jump links (Topics, Favorites, Recent, About, Newsletter) that smoothly scroll down the page. All site links live in the footer, so there’s no Menu button. On the real site this is plain anchor links with CSS smooth scrolling.
    • On phones the motto crossfades through its three lines; on desktop it sits centered under the header. The five topics use short labels (Habits, Focus, Faith, Money, Tech) at every width: icon tiles on phones, pills on desktop.
    • Tap the hammers to see the logo strike. It also plays once on page load. The theme button swaps sun and moon and really switches the theme.
    • Your motto sits in small type just under the header. Your photo appears once, in the About block. The signup has an envelope that opens and lifts a letter when tapped, and again on subscribe.
    • The signup is rebuilt: a visible label, one field and button, a plain-language promise, an inline error for a mistyped address, and a confirmation that mirrors Kit’s double opt-in. It still posts to the same Kit form and works without JavaScript.
    • Every animation is skipped for visitors who set “reduce motion”.

    Until you’ve experienced deep grief personally, it is hard to imagine what someone who is grieving is walking through. But once you’ve experienced grief—as it is all but certainly guaranteed if you’ve ever loved in any way—you’ll realize that you’ve probably let people around you down. It’s not too late to say something—the door is still open.

    Say something. Show up. Don’t try to fix anything. If you do those three things, you’re going to be a desperately needed lifeline to a loved one overwhelmed with the waves of grief crashing down all around them.

      The Library: design notes

      Closest to James Clear’s articles page. A plain statement of purpose, three hand-picked favorites, then a topic index you can scan in seconds.

      • Hero keeps your own words: your homepage headline and your motto from the site description.
      • Reader favorites and the topic index come from one data file (data/home.yaml + data/collections.yaml), so you curate in YAML and never edit templates.
      • Topic rows deliberately aren’t cards. Rules and type do the separating, so the page reads like a contents page.
      • Recent posts stay chronological with no dates, only topic and reading time.
      • The mobile menu is a native <details> element, so it needs no JavaScript.
      • Lowest effort of the three, and the safest first ship.

      The Edition: design notes

      A magazine front page. One lead essay gets the stage, a quote band breaks the rhythm, and your three pillars become three doors.

      • The pillar names and subtitles are lifted from your About page (“Following Jesus faithfully”, “Building with purpose”, “Leveraging the right tools”).
      • The lead essay is set by one front-matter flag (lead: true). If nothing is flagged, it falls back to the newest essay.
      • The newsletter reads as a personal letter, built from sentences on your About page.
      • Strongest editorial personality, but it looks stale if the lead never changes. Best if you publish or re-feature at least monthly.
      • Widest typographic range, so it tests whether Playfair Display SC can carry big display sizes.

      The Field Guide: design notes

      Built around reader needs. Visitors choose what they’re working on and get a short collection in a suggested reading order.

      • Seven collections built only from existing articles, with no new writing required beyond one-line descriptions.
      • In Hugo the chips would be plain links to /topics/habits/ and so on. The in-place switching here is only for the mockup.
      • The “Guides & reviews” shelf uses a new optional format: front-matter field (guide, review, how-to).
      • The Logos review shows how showLastUpdated would mark a time-sensitive article.
      • The search box in the header makes the archive feel like a library. It needs the smaller search index from PR 10.
      • Most discoverable of the three, and the most curation work up front.

      Baseline

      I inspected jshirk/jshirk-hugo at commit 9023c8a, ran a production build with the same Hugo version the live site reports (0.143.1 extended), and measured jshirk.com with Lighthouse 12.8. The build is clean and fast: 1.5 s, no errors, 1,154 pages. Its 304 posts run from 2009 to 2026: 181 Methodology, 68 Technology, 55 Theology.

      What I found

      • Critical

        Old WordPress URLs return 404

        /blog/2010/11/water/, /2010/11/water/ and /feed/ all return 404 on production, and at least 7 of your own posts still link to about 20 URLs in that date-based format. Any external backlinks in that format are lost today. One Netlify _redirects file fixes it without changing a single current URL.

      • Critical

        Your heading font never loads

        Playfair Display SC is pulled in with an @import that ends up at byte 4,402 of the bundled stylesheet, and browsers ignore an @import that isn’t first. Visitors see their browser’s default serif in italic. The mockups show the font as intended.

      • High

        The logo is 90% of page weight

      Date policy

      • Hide publish dates and word counts on articles, blog lists, category pages, and search results. Keep reading time and your byline.
      • Keep every original date in front matter, RSS pubDate, the sitemap, and BlogPosting.datePublished. The blog stays sorted newest first.
      • Add optional showLastUpdated: true. It shows “Last updated” only when the post also has a lastmod you set by hand after a meaningful revision. Git commit times are not used, so a typo fix never looks like an update. The Logos review is the first candidate.
      • No date is ever rewritten. CI fails a PR that changes an existing date value.

      How to undo anything

      • Before anything changes: I tag today’s site as pre-redesign, so there is always a known-good version to return to.
      • Before merging: every PR gets its own Netlify deploy preview, a private copy of the site at a throwaway URL. Nothing reaches jshirk.com until you merge.
      • Within seconds: Netlify keeps every past deploy. In Deploys, open the previous one and click “Publish deploy”. The live site switches back without a rebuild.
      • Permanently: each PR can be undone with GitHub’s “Revert” button, or git revert, which adds a new commit that cancels it. History is kept and nothing is force-pushed.
      • Low blast radius: PRs are small and ordered, so reverting one (say, the new homepage) leaves the others (redirects, metadata) in place.
